HTSDesk · identity
Four marks, one idea
An HTS code is a hierarchy read as a number — chapter, heading, subheading, statistical line. Every mark below draws that structure rather than a shipping cliché. No containers, no globes, no arrows.
A · INDENT
The schedule itself
Four indents descending to the statistical line, the last one flagged. Literally how the tariff schedule is printed.
Tradeoff: reads as a generic list at 16px.
B · SEGMENTS
6109 · 10
The 4·2·2·2 grouping of a ten-digit code, with the separating dot as the accent. Insiders read it instantly.
Tradeoff: means nothing to a first-time visitor.
C · BRACKET
Scope, bounded
Two brackets form an H and enclose a rule. Classification is deciding what a heading does and does not cover.
Tradeoff: the H is doing more work than the idea.
D · STAMP
Cleared
A customs seal. Warmest and most human of the four, and the only one that signals officialdom.
Tradeoff: implies we certify entries. We do not.
